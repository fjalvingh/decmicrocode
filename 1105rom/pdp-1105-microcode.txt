AUX field. From 1973 schematic: seems to determine whether an ALU operation comes from bits 15..12 or from bits 10..6. It controls 
the bits ALU S0..S3, ALU MODE, CIN H, CLK L and DISAB VBIT ROM H through a PROM mapping the instruction bits
to these signals.
AUX_CONTROL_L=1: the bits come from IR 10..6 (E054)
for AUX_CONTROL_L=0 the bits come from IR 15..12 (E053)

ISSUES TO RESOLVE:

Answerable from EK-KD11B-MM-001 or the schematics

  CKO's exact condition — the last of the 23 fields without a settled meaning. It's the clock stop, but 22 of the
  36 CKO=0 microwords start a bus cycle and 14 don't, while 17 start a DATI without it. Also the value-name
  polarity (OFF=1) would put the clock off in 178 of 214 microwords, which can't be right.
  
  BTP=1, BBT=0 — occurs in 26 microwords and your notes don't name that combination, though it's used more than
  two of the three named ones. +1 (0,0) never occurs at all.
  
  IRS vs IRD in SPAMUX — the data pins ROM and BA (invariant when the mux bits swap) but can't distinguish the
  other two. Taken from the notes as given.
  
  Four value names on trust: ALG's SPR (1 microword) and PSW (5), plus those two.
  
  Why the revision moved AUX — the field is understood now, but not what the E→F change was for. It took
  IR-decoded ALU control away from the six not_A microwords and gave it to the shift terminators and the U group.
  
  Curiosity, no impact
  
  What the five seven-step shift chains do. Seven shifts ending on an eighth step, SBO/SB1/SB2/DO — looks
  byte-wide, but that's a guess.
  
  42 unlisted control-store locations — unused, or used and not printed.
